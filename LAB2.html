Problem Statement

Damped oscillation of a robotic arm

A 10 kg robotic arm end-effector attached to a spring (k = 1 N/m) is displaced 50 m from equilibrium and released. External force: 10 N, damping coefficient: 8.

The motion of a spring-mass-damper system is governed by a second-order linear ODE. This models the position and velocity of a robotic arm under realistic mechanical constraints.

Governing Equation — Spring-Mass-Damper
m(d²x/dt²) + c(dx/dt) + kx = F
m= mass (kg)
c= damping coefficient
k= spring stiffness (N/m)
F= external force (N)
x(t)= displacement from equilibrium

This is a second-order linear ODE representing simple harmonic motion with forcing. The RK4 numerical method is used to integrate the equations of motion over time.

Analytical Solution

Solving the ODE

Characteristic equation approach with particular integral

  1. Substitute values: 10(d²x/dt²) + 8(dx/dt) + 50x = 10
  2. Form characteristic equation: 10d² + 8d + 50 = 0
  3. Apply quadratic formula to find roots d = −0.4 ± 2.2i (complex conjugates → underdamped)
  4. Write complementary solution with decaying cosine/sine terms
  5. Find particular integral by assuming xₚ = A (constant)
  6. Apply initial conditions x(0) = 0.1, x′(0) = 0 to determine C₁ and C₂
Complete Analytical Solution
Characteristic Equation
10d² + 8d + 50 = 0
d = (−8 ± √(64 − 2000)) / 20
Complex Roots → Underdamped
d = −0.4 ± 2.2i
Complementary Solution
xc(t) = e^(−0.4t)(C₁ cos 2.2t + C₂ sin 2.2t)
Particular Solution
10(0) + 8(0) + 50A = 10 → A = 0.2
xₚ = 0.2
General Solution
x(t) = e^(−0.4t)(C₁ cos 2.2t + C₂ sin 2.2t) + 0.2

System Dynamics · Fig 1 & 2

Equilibrium Convergence

The robotic arm starts at 0.10 m and settles at equilibrium of 0.20 m. Velocity peaks at the steepest displacement changes and reaches zero at each oscillation peak, confirming energy loss through damping.

Mass Impact · Higher Mass

Increased Inertia

Higher mass (m) leads to increased inertia, causing larger overshoots and a much slower settling time — the natural frequency drops as mass increases.

Mass Impact · Lower Mass

Faster Settling

Lower mass results in a faster, more stable response with minimal oscillation — reaching the target position quickly (under 5 seconds) with the least vibration.

Conclusion

Optimal Robotic Performance

For optimal robotic performance, a lower mass is ideal as it reaches the target position quickly with the least amount of vibration.

Mass Study

Effect of Mass Variation

Varying m from 5 to 30 kg while keeping k, c, F constant

5 kg
Fastest settling. Minimal overshoot. Reaches equilibrium in under 5 s with negligible oscillation.
10 kg
Baseline case. Moderate damped oscillations with a settling time of approximately 10–12 s.
20 kg
Noticeably lower oscillation frequency. Larger overshoots. Takes around 15 s to settle.
30 kg
Slowest response. Largest inertia. Still oscillating significantly at t = 17.5 s in the simulation.
Natural Frequency Formula
ωₙ = √(k/m)
As mass increases, ωₙ decreases, meaning the system oscillates more slowly. The settling time grows because the damping ratio ζ = c / (2√(km)) also changes.
For baseline m = 10 kg
ωₙ = √(50/10) ≈ 2.24 rad/s
Conclusion

RK4 Successfully Models Damped Oscillation

The spring-mass-damper system was modelled as a second-order ODE and solved numerically using RK4. The simulation accurately captured the underdamped oscillation of the robotic arm, confirming analytical predictions. The mass variation study showed clearly that lower mass systems settle faster — a key design insight for precision robotic applications where rapid, stable motion is essential.
