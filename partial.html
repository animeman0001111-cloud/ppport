What is a PDE?

Multi-variable change

Unlike ODEs, PDEs involve functions of two or more independent variables — essential for describing heat, waves, and fields in physical space.

A Partial Differential Equation (PDE) is an equation involving partial derivatives of a function with respect to two or more independent variables.

Unlike ordinary differential equations (ODEs), PDEs deal with functions of multiple variables, making them far more powerful — and complex — tools for modeling physical phenomena.

General Form
F(x, y, z, ∂z/∂x, ∂z/∂y, ∂²z/∂x², ∂²z/∂y², ...) = 0
x, y= independent variables
z= dependent variable
∂z/∂x= partial derivative

PDEs are widely used to model physical phenomena such as heat transfer, wave propagation, fluid flow, electromagnetics, and engineering systems. They describe how quantities change across space and time simultaneously.

Distinction

ODE vs. PDE

Key differences between ordinary and partial differential equations

Aspect ODE (Ordinary D.E.) PDE (Partial D.E.)
Derivative Type Contains ordinary derivatives Contains partial derivatives
Variables One independent variable Two or more independent variables
Complexity Simpler to solve More complex
Example dy/dx = x + y ∂u/∂t = α ∂²u/∂x²

The Heat Equation

Describing how temperature evolves over time and space

Heat Equation
∂u/∂t = α ∂²u/∂x²
u= Temperature
t= Time
x= Position
α= Thermal Diffusivity

Heat flows from regions of higher temperature to regions of lower temperature. Over time, the temperature distribution becomes more uniform as energy spreads through the medium.

This principle applies to heat exchangers, engine cooling systems, electronic device cooling, and thermal insulation design.

  • Initial temperature difference is high
  • Heat spreads gradually through the medium
  • Temperature becomes uniform over time

The Wave Equation

Modelling vibrations and propagating disturbances through media

Wave Equation
∂²u/∂t² = c² ∂²u/∂x²
u= Displacement
c= Wave Speed

The wave equation governs the propagation of disturbances through elastic media. Energy is transferred from particle to particle while each particle oscillates about its equilibrium position.

  • String Vibrations & Musical Instruments
  • Sound Waves in Air and Solids
  • Earthquake (Seismic) Analysis
  • Wireless Communication Signals
  • Structural Vibration Analysis

The Laplace Equation

Describing steady-state potential fields with no local extrema

Laplace Equation
∂²u/∂x² + ∂²u/∂y² = 0

The Laplace equation describes potential fields in a region with no sources or sinks. Its solutions are harmonic functions — smooth surfaces without any peaks or valleys inside the domain.

  • Electrostatic Fields
  • Irrotational Fluid Flow
  • Steady-State Heat Transfer
  • Gravitational Potential Fields

Solution Requirements

Boundary Conditions

A PDE requires boundary conditions for a unique, physically meaningful solution

Dirichlet Condition

The value of the function is specified at the boundary. Represents prescribed temperature, displacement, or potential.

u(0, t) = 0

Neumann Condition

The derivative of the function (flux) is specified at the boundary. Represents insulated surfaces or zero-flux conditions.

∂u/∂x = 0

Mixed Condition

A combination of both Dirichlet and Neumann conditions, applied to different parts of the boundary for complex systems.

αu + β ∂u/∂n = g

Boundary conditions represent real-world physical constraints — the temperature at the edge of a rod, the insulated end of a pipe, or the pressure at the wall of a vessel.

Solution Method

Separation of Variables

One of the most powerful techniques for solving PDEs analytically

The method of separation of variables assumes the solution can be written as a product of functions, each depending on only one variable. This transforms the PDE into simpler ODEs.

  1. Assume solution of the form u(x,t) = X(x) · T(t)
  2. Substitute into the PDE
  3. Separate variables — each side equals a constant
  4. Obtain and solve the resulting ODEs individually
  5. Combine solutions to satisfy boundary conditions

Advantages: Simplifies PDE into ODEs; widely used for Heat and Wave equations; mathematically tractable.

Solved Example — Heat Equation
Given
∂u/∂t = ∂²u/∂x²
Assume
u(x,t) = X(x) · T(t)
After Substitution
1/T · dT/dt = 1/X · d²X/dx² = −k
Resulting ODEs
dT/dt + kT = 0
d²X/dx² + kX = 0
Solution approach
Solve individually, then combine with boundary conditions to form the complete solution via Fourier series.
